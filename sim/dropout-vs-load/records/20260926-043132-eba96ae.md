Methodology fixed by issue #71 (superseding #18's original method): the deck sweeps VIN downward, from a comfortably-regulating value toward a value well below the 1.8V target, at a finer (20mV) resolution than #18's original 50mV; dropout_v is the Vin-Vout margin at the VIN where VOUT first falls (`.meas dc ... fall=1`) through 98% of the 1.8V target as VIN decreases -- the classic "regulation just lost" dropout definition -- not a fixed low-VIN endpoint (#18's method reported the pass device's residual V_sd well past dropout, overstating the gap; see #71's PR for the derivation and design/README.md's campaign section for the closed-loop screening that motivated the fix). vout_at_max_vin_v is a light-headroom regulation sanity check at the sweep's top (first-swept) end. INITIAL-CONDITION CONTRACT (#171, converted by #178): the analysis is no longer a `dc VVIN 3.63 1.5 -0.02` sweep. It is ONE cold-start, settled transient ('tran 10u 14m uic') in which VIN is a PWL ramp -- 0V to the sweep's own 3.63V top by 100us, EN stepping high at 100us, the ideal 50mA sink ramped on over 2.5-2.6ms once the loop is up (sim/ic-screen-125c-c's own cold-start convention, verbatim), settled by 5.5ms, then VIN walked DOWN 3.63V->1.5V over 6ms-14ms at 266.3 V/s as a quasi-static line droop -- so there is no operating-point solve in the deck at all: the contract's 'uic' + EN-edge shape, the one PR #170 landed for mc-output-accuracy and #172 for line-/load-regulation. WHY a shape change rather than a seed: ngspice honours neither accepted seed shape for a `dc` analysis.
