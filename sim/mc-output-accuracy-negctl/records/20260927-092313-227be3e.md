- **Claim**: DELIBERATELY BROKEN BY DESIGN -- this experiment makes no claim about the LDO as designed, and its FAIL is the intended result. It is the seeded, known-bad variant that `sim/mc-output-accuracy`'s `klt yield` analysis needs in order to carry a `negative_control` block (klt yield's own mechanism, docs/cli/yield.md#negative-control; requested by T1 signoff item 6's checklist text and tracked as issue #211): a yield statistic that has never been shown to reject a degraded design is an assumption, not evidence. The variant is `sim/mc-output-accuracy`'s bench, verbatim -- same testbench schematic (referenced, not copied), same mismatch corner point (tt_mm/27C/VIN=3.3V, I_LOAD=1mA), same initial-condition contract (`uic` plus the 100us EN edge, #164), same measurement (`vout_ss`), same ratified 1.764-1.836V window, same seed -- with exactly one declared defect applied to the netlisted deck: the feedback divider's top leg XR_FB_A is lengthened 180um -> 198um (+10%). WHAT WAS DEGRADED AND WHY THAT: the divider is three nominally identical sky130_fd_pr__res_xhigh_po legs (W=0.42, L=180um each) tapping FB at 2/3 of VOUT, so the ideal regulation point is VREF*(R_A+R_B+R_C)/(R_B+R_C) = 1.2V*3/2 = 1.8V. A ratio error in that divider is the canonical accuracy defect for an LDO and is precisely what a ratified +-2% Output row exists to catch; +10% on the top leg moves the ideal point to 1.2V*558/360 = 1.86V, i.e. +3.33%, which is 1.67x the ratified window and sits ~2.8 sigma beyond the window's upper edge in the nominal campaign's OWN measured mismatch sigma (8.52mV, klt yield record sim/mc-output-accuracy/records/20260927-030409-1a14401.md). That magnitude is a deliberate choice in both directions: large enough that a statistic with any detection power must reject it, and small enough that the loop still regulates, so the draws stay measurable and the control tests yield DETECTION rather than producing an avalanche of errored samples. It also degrades the design (the on-chip divider), not the testbench stimulus, and it does not depend on resistor-family mismatch being sampled -- `klt sim` reports resistor mismatch activity as `unconfirmed` for sky130 -- so the defect is deterministic and the only stochastic ingredient is the same MOS mismatch the nominal campaign samples. HOW THE DEFECT IS EXPRESSED: as this manifest's `netlist_patch` block, an exact-string substitution with an asserted occurrence count, applied by sim/bin/mc-run.py to the netlisted deck between xschem and `klt sim`. `design/ldo_3v3in_1v8out.sch` is untouched (it stays the single source of truth for the design under test), and no frozen 60-device copy of it is introduced that could silently drift; if the divider in the real design ever changes, the asserted count stops matching and this campaign refuses to run rather than quietly sampling an undegraded circuit and reporting it as a negative control. WHY N=40 AND NOT THE NOMINAL'S 200: the control's job is to demonstrate detection power, and the sample count that job needs follows from the interval arithmetic `klt yield` actually applies. The nominal's 200-of-200 in-window draws support an exact (Clopper-Pearson) empirical yield of 'at least 98.1725% at 95% confidence'; a `detected` verdict requires the control's own exact interval not to overlap that, and a control that fails every draw reaches a CP upper bound of 1-0.025^(1/40) = 8.8% at N=40 -- an order of magnitude clear of the nominal's lower bound, with headroom for a control that does NOT fail every draw. N=200 would buy a tighter estimate of a number that is not the point, at 5x the compute on a machine this campaign has to share; N=40 is chosen against the criterion, not against the nominal.
